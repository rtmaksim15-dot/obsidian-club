### Attorney-review footnotes

[^age]: Confirm whether self-attestation plus third-party age verification satisfies each applicable **state age-verification statute** (post *FSC v. Paxton*, June 2025). Decide whether verification is required for all users or geo-targeted to covered states, and whether the "harmful to minors" threshold reaches a private, invite-only community.
[^sponsor]: Confirm sponsorship record does not create agency, fiduciary, or negligent-referral exposure between members, and that recording it does not create defamation/privacy risk. Consider a member-to-member release.
[^license]: Confirm license scope is narrow enough to reassure members yet broad enough to operate/moderate; align deletion mechanics with the Privacy Policy retention schedule and any legal-hold obligations.
[^mod]: "Rights not duties" framing is intended to preserve Section 230(c) protection and avoid converting moderation into contractual promises. Verify consistency with FOSTA-SESTA posture (see Section 230 memo).
[^payments]: Confirm the invite-with-purchase mechanic is not recharacterized as paying for access to adult content under any state consumer-protection or adult-commerce statute; keep goods sale and Platform access legally separate.
[^redline]: "No appeal / no cure" is enforceable as a contract term but should remain discretionary. Confirm it does not conflict with any statutory notice or reinstatement duty and that preservation/reporting steps match §2258A (NCMEC) obligations.
[^asis]: Confirm disclaimer conspicuousness (all-caps/bold) meets California standards; ensure it does not disclaim liability for gross negligence, willful misconduct, or non-waivable consumer protections.
[^liability]: Validate the cap and exclusions under California law; some consumer protections and certain claims (e.g., personal injury in some contexts) may not be limitable. Consider a higher/typical cap for credibility.
[^classwaiver]: Class-action waivers are generally enforceable under the FAA (*Concepcion*, *Epic Systems*), but confirm mass-arbitration cost allocation and any provider-specific mass-filing rules to avoid a fee-shifting trap.
[^mcgill]: The California *McGill v. Citibank* rule bars waiver of public injunctive relief; the carve-out is drafted to preserve it. Counsel to finalize.
[^arbsever]: Consider whether a "blowup"/non-severability clause (whole arbitration agreement voids if class waiver fails) is preferable to piecemeal severance — strategic call.
[^gov]: If the entity forms in Delaware, decide whether to keep California governing law (operator/consumer nexus) or choose Delaware with a California carve-out for non-waivable consumer protections.
